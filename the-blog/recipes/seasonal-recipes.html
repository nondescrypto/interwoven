Seasonal Recipes

— Spring —

"Spring is a perfect time to prepare lighter and simpler meals packed with nutritious flavor.

The greener the vegetable, the more nutrient dense it is, and exactly what we need after a long winter of comfort foods. All you have to do is grab a handful of greens and top off any dish raw or cooked. Choose from a variety of deep green foods such as kale, spinach, pea tendrils, red and green lettuces, and asparagus. There are still so many more varieties out there but it depends on what your local farmer is growing each season.

Many greens at the farmers market already come in bags and just need a quick wash. It is always a good idea to put your greens in a bowl filled with water and a cap full of apple cider vinegar, give it a swish, drain, pat dry with a paper towel and store in a breathable container in the refrigerator. Leafy greens cook within a minute, asparagus takes a little longer."

— Rhonda

Green Omelet

A simple, flexible omelet packed with greens and asparagus. Easily adaptable based on what vegetables you have on hand.

Serves: 1

Ingredients

  • 2-3 eggs, beaten
  • 1 tablespoon light cream or milk
  • 1 teaspoon butter
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 2 asparagus spears, chopped
  • 1 handful leafy greens (spinach, kale, or similar)
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Cheese, optional

Instructions

  1. In a small bowl, whisk together the eggs and cream. Season lightly with salt and pepper.
  2. Heat butter and olive oil in a small skillet over medium heat. Add the asparagus and cook for about 2 minutes. Add greens and stir until just wilted.
  3. Pour in the egg mixture, tilting the pan to spread evenly. Cook until the edges begin to set and the bottom is lightly golden.
  4. Gently flip or fold the omelet. Cook for another 30–60 seconds, or until the eggs are fully set but still tender. Add cheese before folding, if using.
  5. Serve warm with toast or fruit on the side.

Variation (Baked Option)

Mix chopped asparagus and leafy greens into the egg mixture. Pour the mixture into greased muffin cups and bake at 350°F until set and lightly golden. These can be cooled, wrapped individually, and frozen for up to 2 months. Reheat gently at 250°F or until warmed through.

Warm Spinach Salad with Mushrooms & Roasted Root Vegetables

A flexible, hearty salad that uses up leftover vegetables and turns them into a warm, satisfying meal. The heat from the roasted vegetables gently wilts the spinach.

Serves: 2-3

Ingredients

  • 2-3 cups roasted vegetables (such as beets, potatoes, carrots, parsnips, turnips, radishes, sweet potatoes, and onion)
  • 2 mushrooms, sliced
  • 2 handfuls fresh spinach, washed
  • Olive oil
  • Balsamic vinegar or balsamic glaze
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Fresh parsley or rosemary, chopped
  • Optional: feta cheese for topping

Instructions

  1. Preheat oven to 350°F. Cut vegetables into even pieces and place in a bowl. Drizzle with olive oil and toss to coat. Spread on a parchment-lined baking sheet.
  2. Roast until fork-tender, stirring occasionally. (Timing will vary depending on the vegetables used.)
  3. Add the sliced mushrooms and herbs partway through roasting. Toss gently and continue roasting until the mushrooms are tender.
  4. Remove from the oven and transfer the warm vegetables to a large bowl.
  5. Add the spinach and drizzle with olive oil and balsamic vinegar or glaze. Toss gently so the heat from the vegetables wilts the spinach.
  6. Season with salt and pepper to taste. Top with feta cheese if desired.

Serving Tip

Serve warm as a light lunch or dinner on its own.

Variations

  • Serve leftover roasted vegetables over quinoa with olive oil, lemon juice, zest, parsley, and basil.
  • For a different flavor profile, use cilantro, lime, and spices like cumin or chili powder.
  • Turn leftovers into a baked casserole by mixing vegetables with a little cream, shredded cheese (such as cheddar, Colby, or Monterey Jack), herbs, and spices. Bake in a buttered dish until bubbly and lightly golden.

Pasta with Mushrooms & Spinach

A simple, comforting pasta dish with sautéed mushrooms, fresh spinach, and a light lemon finish.

Serves: 2

Ingredients

  • 6 oz dried pasta (about 2 cups dry), such as spaghetti, penne, or linguine
  • 1 handful mushrooms, sliced (or more, to taste)
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • 1 small shallot, sliced
  • 2 handfuls fresh spinach
  • Olive oil
  • Zest of ½ lemon (or to taste)
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Parmesan cheese, optional
  • Pinch of red pepper flakes, optional
  • Reserved pasta water (about 1 cup)

Instructions

  1. Cook pasta according to package directions. Before draining, reserve about 1 cup of pasta water. Drain and set aside.
  2. While the pasta cooks, heat olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add mushrooms, garlic, and shallot. Sauté until the mushrooms are golden and softened.
  3. Remove the pan from heat and stir in the spinach until just wilted.
  4. Add the cooked pasta to the skillet.
  5. Add lemon zest and a pinch of red pepper flakes if using. Toss to combine, adding reserved pasta water a little at a time to help create a light sauce. Drizzle in a little olive oil as needed to coat the pasta.
  6. Season with salt and pepper to taste. Toss again until well combined.
  7. Top with Parmesan cheese, if desired, and serve warm with a simple salad.

La Rhonda's Rhubarb Soda

A bright, tart homemade syrup made with fresh rhubarb and lemon. Mix with sparkling water for a refreshing seasonal drink.

Makes about 1½–2 cups syrup · Yields approximately 12–16 drinks

Rhubarb Simple Syrup — Ingredients

  • 2 cups water
  • 1 cup sugar
  • 1 cup diced rhubarb
  • Juice of 1 lemon

Instructions

  1. In a medium saucepan, combine water, sugar, rhubarb, and lemon juice.
  2. Bring to a gentle simmer over medium heat, stirring occasionally until the sugar dissolves.
  3. Simmer for about 10–15 minutes, until the rhubarb softens and the liquid turns a light pink color.
  4. Remove from heat and strain through a fine mesh strainer, pressing gently on the solids to extract flavor.
  5. Let cool completely. Store in a glass jar or bottle in the refrigerator for up to 1 week.

To Serve

Mix 2–4 tablespoons of syrup with soda water, seltzer, or mineral water to taste.

Serving Tip

Serve over ice and garnish with strawberries, lime, or other citrus.

Cook's Note

This syrup can also be used in cocktails or mocktails. Adjust sweetness by adding more or less syrup depending on your preference.

The Sicilian Sauté

A simple, fresh vegetable sauté that highlights summer produce with olive oil, herbs, and garlic. Serve as a side dish or over pasta or rice for a light meal.

Serves: 1-2 (or 2-3 as a side dish)

Ingredients

  • 1 small zucchini, sliced
  • 1 small summer squash, sliced
  • ½ red bell pepper, quartered and sliced
  • ½ small onion, sliced
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • Olive oil, for sautéing
  • Fresh basil or parsley, to taste
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Parmesan cheese, for serving (optional)

Instructions

  1. Heat a drizzle of olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and onion and sauté until softened and translucent.
  2. Add the zucchini, summer squash, and bell pepper. Stir to combine. Cook for a few minutes, stirring occasionally, until the vegetables begin to soften and release moisture.
  3. Continue cooking until the vegetables are tender but still slightly firm (not mushy) and lightly golden in spots.
  4. Season with salt and pepper, then add fresh herbs and toss to combine.
  5. Serve warm as-is, over pasta or rice, or as a side dish. Top with pesto or a sprinkle of Parmesan if desired.

Serving Tip

This makes a simple, flavorful side for grilled or roasted meats, or a light vegetarian meal over grains or pasta.

Veggie Fritters

Crispy, savory fritters made with fresh corn and vegetables. Great as a light meal, side dish, or tucked into wraps and tacos.

Serves: 2-3 (as a light meal or side)

Ingredients

  • 2 cups corn kernels (from about 2 fresh cobs)
  • ½ onion, red or white, finely chopped
  • ½ bell pepper, finely chopped
  • 1 carrot, grated
  • Fresh herbs (cilantro, parsley, or basil), to taste
  • Juice of ½ lime (with cilantro) or ½ lemon (with basil)
  • Zest of ½ citrus fruit
  • ½ to 1 cup flour (all-purpose or gluten-free)
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Olive oil, for frying

Instructions

  1. In a mixing bowl, combine all ingredients. Stir until well mixed. The batter should be thick and sticky—able to hold its shape when scooped. If it is too loose, add a little more flour, a tablespoon at a time.
  2. Heat a skillet over medium heat and add a thin layer of olive oil.
  3. Drop spoonfuls of batter into the pan using a tablespoon. Flatten slightly if needed.
  4. Cook until golden brown on both sides, about 2–3 minutes per side.
  5. Transfer to a paper towel-lined plate to drain excess oil.

Serving Tip

Serve warm with yogurt sauce, and add hot sauce if desired.

Variation

Shape into larger patties for veggie burgers, or cut into halves to use in tacos or wraps.

Storage Tip

Cooked fritters can be refrigerated for up to 3 days. Reheat in a skillet or oven to maintain crispness.

Freezer Tip

Freeze cooked fritters in a single layer, then transfer to a container or bag. Reheat from frozen in a skillet or oven until warmed through and crisp.

Southcoast Sicilian Seafood Stew

A simple, coastal-inspired tomato and seafood stew that comes together quickly and is best served with crusty bread.

Serves: 2-3

Ingredients

  • 8 oz white fish (such as cod), or other seafood of choice
  • 2 medium tomatoes or 1 (8 oz) can tomato sauce
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • Olive oil, for sautéing
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Fresh basil, chopped
  • ½–1 cup water, as needed

Instructions

  1. Heat a small amount of olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and sauté for about 2 minutes.
  2. Add the tomato sauce or chopped tomatoes and simmer for about 10 minutes. If using fresh tomatoes, add about ½ cup water to help them break down.
  3. Add the fish and simmer gently until cooked through and flaky, adding more water as needed to maintain a light broth.
  4. Season with salt and pepper to taste.
  5. Stir in fresh basil and simmer briefly until the flavors come together.
  6. Adjust consistency as desired—simmer longer for a thicker stew or keep it more brothy if preferred.

Serving Tip

Serve warm with crusty bread for dipping.

Variation

Add shellfish or squid for a mixed seafood version. Cook shellfish separately until opened, then add to the tomato base along with any cooking liquid (avoiding any grit at the bottom of the pan). Fresh corn kernels also make a nice addition.

Freezer Tip

This stew is best enjoyed fresh. If freezing, do so without shellfish and note that the texture of fish may soften slightly when reheated.

La Rhonda's Blueberry Soda

A refreshing homemade soda made with fresh blueberries and lemon. Mix with sparkling water for a bright, seasonal drink.

Ceviche de Pulpo

Octopus ceviche brightened with lime, sweet peppers, and cilantro — the acid of the lime cooks the meat, just like the other ingredients finish the dish. Served with a side of sweet potato and corn as close as you can get to the authenticity of the Peruvian dish.

Serves: 2-3

Ingredients

  • 1 to 2 pounds octopus meat, thawed in fridge, legs only (remove and discard head and beak, may be used)
  • 1 cup freshly squeezed lime juice
  • 1 small red onion (shallot or scallion may be used)
  • 1 thinly sliced hot pepper of choice, or mild sweet bell pepper
  • A few sprigs of fresh cilantro, to taste
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • ¼ to ½ cup extra virgin olive oil (add oil just before serving)

Instructions

  1. Bring a medium pot of water to a boil. Drop in the octopus and blanch for one minute. Remove from pot and submerge in ice cold water until cool.
  2. Slice the legs into quarter-inch pieces, put in a large bowl.
  3. Add lime juice. Add more to evenly coat the octopus slices if needed. Add the rest of the ingredients except the oil.
  4. Toss, cover, and place in the refrigerator for 2 hours.
  5. Add oil before serving. Toss and season to taste.
  6. Spoon ceviche into a bowl and serve with tortillas and guacamole.

Serving Options

As a light lunch or dinner, serve with corn on the cob and sweet potato. This is a popular combo in Peru!

Please Note

Consuming raw or undercooked meats, poultry, seafood, shellfish, or eggs may increase your risk of foodborne illness, especially if you have certain medical conditions.

Note

If you cannot find raw octopus, then cooked frozen can be used and the dish becomes more of a ceviche-style versus a straightforward ceviche.

Deep Purple “Stuffies” — Baked Clams on the Half Shell

Local top neck quahog clams, simmered open, then stuffed with a lightly toasted breadcrumb filling and baked until golden — a South Coast summer classic, inspired by a lifetime of digging for quahogs with family.

Serves: 6 people, two clams each

Ingredients

  • 12 raw top neck quahog clams (be sure all the clams are closed — if the shells are open, they are not safe for consumption, though you may want to save the shell for its color. Scrub the excess mud off the clam and rinse.)
  • 2 teaspoons of salt
  • 2 quarts of water
  • 2 tablespoons of corn meal
  • 1 tablespoon of butter
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 1 whole garlic clove
  • 1 cup of breadcrumbs (Italian seasoning or plain) or Ritz crackers if preferred
  • 1 teaspoon freshly chopped flat-leaf parsley
  • Salt and pepper, to taste
  • Hot pepper flakes, if desired
  • One lemon (to include 1 teaspoon of lemon juice and 6 lemon wedges)
  • 1 to 2 sprigs of flat-leaf parsley

Instructions

  1. Soak the clams in a saltwater solution of 2 teaspoons of salt, two quarts of water, and 2 tablespoons of corn meal for 30 to 60 minutes.
  2. Put clams in a bowl with fresh, running water to rinse.
  3. Place the clams in a skillet with an inch or two of water, cover, and simmer until the clams open, 5 to 10 minutes. Remove and allow to cool.
  4. Save the clam juice from simmering and pour into a large jar or measuring cup, allowing the sediment to settle. Set aside.
  5. In a skillet on medium heat, melt the butter and olive oil gently. Do not let it burn or smoke. Add minced shallot and the whole garlic clove, sauté until translucent, then remove the garlic clove. Add the breadcrumbs and toast lightly. Add clam juice to moisten as needed, ¼ cup at a time to avoid making a soggy mess. (Freeze the remainder of the clam juice for another recipe.)
  6. Mix and season with salt and pepper to taste. Add hot pepper flakes if desired.
  7. Assemble the clam shells on a baking sheet by separating the shells, keeping the whole clams intact. With a knife, release the muscle if needed, and place the clams in a small bowl.
  8. Season the clams with a light drizzle of olive oil, 1 tablespoon of lemon juice, a pinch of salt and pepper, and 1 teaspoon of freshly chopped flat-leaf parsley. Toss to coat and place in shells. Top with the breadcrumb mixture to evenly distribute the crumbs and bake for 20 to 25 minutes, or until golden brown and toasty.
  9. Serve with one fresh lemon wedge per plate and top with a sprinkle of fresh parsley.

Disfruta! Enjoy!

Bittersweet Autumn Soup

A flexible, hearty soup designed for using up leftover vegetables. Warm, comforting, and easy to adapt based on what you have on hand.

Serves: 3-4

Ingredients

  • Leftover vegetables such as corn, carrots, green beans, potatoes, peppers, onion, garlic, tomato, mushrooms, or similar
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 teaspoon Herbs de Provence or other dried herbs
  • Water, enough to cover vegetables
  • Better Than Bouillon, to taste
  • 2 tablespoons Bragg's Liquid Aminos
  • Black pepper, to taste
  • Optional: cooked rice, barley, or small pasta

Instructions

  1. Heat olive oil in a large pot over medium heat. Add chopped vegetables and cook for about 10 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  2. Add Herbs de Provence and cook for 2 more minutes.
  3. Pour in enough water to just cover the vegetables. Stir in Better Than Bouillon, Liquid Aminos, and black pepper.
  4. Cover and simmer for about 30 minutes, or until vegetables are tender. Add more water if needed.
  5. If using a starch (rice, barley, or pasta), cook it separately, rinse, and stir into the soup just before serving.

Serving Tip

Serve warm with bread and a simple salad for a complete meal.

Freezer Tip

Cool completely and freeze leftovers in single portions for easy future meals.

La Rhonda's Rustic Marinara Sauce

Tomatoes are dwindling in the late summer and early fall, so you can still enjoy them through this simple, fresh tomato sauce made with summer tomatoes, garlic, and basil. Perfect tossed with pasta or served alongside bread.

Serves: 2-3

Ingredients

  • 4–6 fresh summer tomatoes (from the vine or local farmers market), diced
  • 2 cloves garlic, chopped
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 2–3 tablespoons olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • Red pepper flakes (optional)
  • Fresh basil (a handful), chopped plus extra whole leaves for garnish
  • 6–8 oz pasta of choice

Instructions

  1. Heat olive oil in a skillet over medium heat. Add the garlic and onion.
  2. Sauté until softened and translucent, about 5 minutes. Do not let them brown.
  3. Add the diced tomatoes, salt, pepper, and red pepper flakes if using. Stir to combine.
  4. Bring to a gentle simmer, then reduce heat to low-medium. Cook for about 20 minutes, stirring occasionally.
  5. If the sauce becomes too thick, add a small splash of water as needed.
  6. Stir in chopped basil just before serving, reserving whole leaves for garnish.
  7. Toss with cooked pasta and serve warm.

Serving Tip

Pasta shapes with ridges or curves work best, as they hold onto the sauce. Top with Parmesan cheese if desired.

Cook's Note

This sauce is best made with peak-season summer tomatoes for the richest flavor.

Sweet Tart Apple Crisp

This simple fruit crisp is warmly spiced with a bright tart flavor from the cranberries. It makes a cozy dessert for chilly evenings and is just as lovely served for breakfast the next morning.

Serves: 2 (or 1-2 depending on portion size)

Ingredients

  • 2 apples, peeled and diced or sliced
  • ¼ cup cranberries
  • Pinches of cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves, to taste
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • ¼–½ cup water
  • Maple syrup, to taste
  • 1 tablespoon sugar, optional
  • Oats, for topping

Instructions

  1. In a sauté pan over medium heat, melt the butter. Add the apples, cranberries, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. Cook for about 2 minutes.
  2. Add the water and drizzle with maple syrup to taste. Add sugar, if desired.
  3. Continue cooking until the fruit is tender and the cranberries begin to burst and release their color. Let the liquid cook down slightly until syrupy.
  4. In a separate pan, toast the oats over medium heat with a little butter and a sprinkle of the spices. Drizzle with maple syrup near the end of cooking and continue to toast until lightly golden.
  5. Spoon the fruit mixture into bowls and top with the toasted oats.

Serving Tip

Serve warm with whipped cream, a drizzle of heavy cream, or a scoop of ice cream.

Variation

For a warm breakfast, serve with yogurt instead of cream or ice cream. You can also replace the cranberries with roasted butternut squash for a cozy fall variation.

Thanksgiving Shepherd's Pie

A cozy way to turn Thanksgiving leftovers into a brand-new meal. This comforting casserole is flexible, easy to assemble, and perfect for a quiet supper the day after the holiday.

Serves: 2-4

Ingredients

  • Leftover cooked turkey, cut into bite-sized pieces
  • Leftover vegetables, or peas and carrots
  • Leftover gravy
  • Savory holiday leftovers, as desired
  • Leftover mashed potatoes, mashed sweet potatoes, or mashed butternut squash
  • Homemade cranberry sauce, for serving

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Butter a small casserole dish or baking dish.
  2. In a large bowl, gently combine the turkey, vegetables, gravy, and any other savory leftovers. Avoid adding sweet dishes such as candied vegetables or sweet casseroles.
  3. Spoon the mixture into the prepared baking dish.
  4. Spread the mashed potatoes, sweet potatoes, or butternut squash evenly over the top.
  5. Bake for 25–30 minutes, or until hot and bubbly and the top is lightly browned.
  6. Serve warm with a spoonful of cranberry sauce drizzled over the top.

Cook's Tip

This recipe is very forgiving—use whatever savory Thanksgiving leftovers you have on hand to make it your own.

Freezer Tip

Freeze extra portions for an easy meal later in the season. Reheat in the oven or microwave until warmed through.

Acorn Flour — Cold Leaching Method

Inspired by Euell Gibbons. A foraged flour made from acorns, leached of their bitter tannic acid, then dried and ground — use it in a 1:1 ratio with regular white flour in your favorite baking recipes.

Yield: varies by batch

Ingredients

  • Fresh acorns, ideally from a White Oak tree (least bitter)
  • Water

Instructions

  1. Collect acorns. (All acorns are good to use but need to be leached of their tannic acid to become edible.) Remove any with cracks or holes. Put the acorns in a bowl and fill with water. Remove any that float.
  2. Dry, then hammer to open. Discard the shells. Transfer the acorn meat to a bowl.
  3. Put 2 cups of the acorns in a blender or food processor to coarsely grind, adding a little water as needed until finely chopped. Rinse through a colander lined with a fine cloth, squeezing and repeating several times. Taste for bitterness and keep rinsing until it should do.
  4. On a parchment-lined cookie sheet, dry the leached acorns in a slow oven set at 250°F until dry. Drying time varies depending on the size of the acorn bits — check after 20 minutes the first time, then every 10 to 15 minutes afterward. Document your time, because every oven is different.
  5. Return the dried acorn meal to the blender or food processor and mix until finely ground.
  6. Store in a glass jar in the refrigerator, or freeze in measured amounts to accommodate recipe conversion.
  7. Use acorn flour in a 1:1 ratio with regular white flour in your favorite baking recipes.

Note

Instead of cold leaching, you can roughly chop the acorns and boil them to leach out the bitterness. Boil and drain until the water is clear and the bitterness is gone, then follow the drying and grinding steps above.

Author's Note

Use Peterson and Golden guides to cross-reference plant identification. When foraging, always use three references. One should always be aware of potentially poisonous plants from your region.

Happy foraging! Disfruta!

American Pumpkin Bread AKA Pan Americano de Calabaza

A warmly spiced pumpkin bread that's rich, tender, and perfect for fall mornings or afternoon coffee. Add pecans and golden raisins for extra texture and sweetness.

Serves: 10-12 (or 1-2 small loaves / about 12 muffins—to freeze and use later)

Ingredients

  • 2 cups all-purpose flour
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 teaspoon ground nutmeg
  • ¼ teaspoon ground cloves
  • ¾ cup (1½ sticks) unsalted butter, softened
  • 2 cups granulated sugar
  • 2 large eggs
  • 2 cups pumpkin purée (canned or homemade)
  • Chopped pecans and golden raisins, optional

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 325°F. Position a rack in the center of the oven. Grease muffin tins or small loaf pans and lightly dust with flour.
  2. In a medium bowl, whisk together the flour, salt, baking soda, baking powder, cinnamon, nutmeg, and cloves. Set aside.
  3. In a large bowl, cream the butter and sugar together until light and fluffy. Add the eggs one at a time, beating well after each addition.
  4. Stir in the pumpkin purée until fully combined. Gradually add the dry ingredients, mixing just until incorporated. Fold in pecans and raisins, if using.
  5. Divide the batter evenly among the prepared pans. Bake for 65–75 minutes, or until a toothpick inserted into the center comes out clean. Smaller pans or muffins may require less baking time.
  6. Let cool in the pans for 10 minutes before transferring to a wire rack to cool completely.

Storage Tip

Wrap the cooled bread tightly in parchment paper, then foil, and freeze for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the refrigerator before serving.

Cook's Notes

  • Homemade pumpkin purée adds a deeper, richer flavor to this bread. Simply halve a sugar pumpkin, scoop out the seeds, and roast cut-side down at 400°F until tender, about 45–60 minutes. Scoop out the flesh and blend until smooth. Be sure to drain any excess liquid before baking.
  • For extra flavor, try soaking the raisins in rum or whiskey before adding them to the batter. Drain well before mixing in.

Variation

This recipe also works beautifully with other winter squashes. Try substituting the pumpkin purée with roasted and puréed butternut squash, acorn squash, or blue hubbard squash for slightly different flavors and sweetness.

Mini Meatloaf Muffins

These mini meatloaves are comforting, easy to portion, and perfect for freezing for future meals. Serve with mashed potatoes, roasted vegetables, or tucked into a sandwich the next day.

Serves: 4 (makes 8-10 mini meatloaf muffins—to freeze for use later)

Ingredients

  • 1 pound ground beef
  • ⅓ cup breadcrumbs (plain, Italian-style, or panko)
  • 1 small onion, finely chopped
  • 1 teaspoon garlic, minced
  • ⅓ cup whole milk, heavy cream, or half-and-half
  • 1 egg, lightly beaten
  • ½ teaspoon salt
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • ¼ cup fresh parsley, chopped
  • Ketchup or barbecue sauce, optional

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F. Lightly grease a muffin pan or line with muffin liners.
  2. In a large bowl, combine the ground beef, breadcrumbs, onion, garlic, milk, egg, salt, pepper, and parsley. Mix gently until just combined. Avoid overmixing, which can make the meatloaf tough.
  3. Divide the mixture evenly among the muffin cups, filling each about ¾ full. If desired, spread a small spoonful of ketchup or barbecue sauce on top of each muffin.
  4. Bake for 20–25 minutes, or until the internal temperature reaches 165°F.
  5. Let rest for 5 minutes before serving.

Freezer Tip

Freeze extra meatloaf muffins individually for easy future meals. Reheat in the microwave or oven until warmed through.

Summer in Winter Soup

Bright flavors and warming spices make this comforting soup feel like a little taste of summer during the colder months. Enjoy with crusty bread, rice, or a simple salad.

Serves: 3 cups

Ingredients

  • 3 small or 2 medium potatoes, peeled and cut into 1-inch cubes
  • 1 sweet pepper or chili pepper, minced
  • 1 garlic clove, finely chopped
  • 1 small onion, shallot, leek, or scallion, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons oil (olive, safflower, or coconut oil)
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric
  • ½ teaspoon paprika
  • ¼ teaspoon cayenne pepper
  • ¼ teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 cup frozen corn or peas
  • 2 cups water, or enough to cover the vegetables
  • 1 can coconut milk or 1 cup milk, optional
  • Juice and zest of ½ lime
  • Chopped cilantro and sliced scallions, for serving
  • Pinch of garam masala, optional

Instructions

  1. Heat the oil in a medium pot over medium heat. Add the potatoes, pepper, garlic, and onion. Cook for about 3–5 minutes, stirring occasionally, until lightly softened.
  2. Stir in the cumin, turmeric, paprika, and cayenne. Cook for 1 minute, until fragrant.
  3. Add the frozen corn or peas and stir for another minute.
  4. Pour in the water, making sure the vegetables are covered. Bring to a simmer and cook on low heat for about 10–15 minutes, or until the potatoes are tender.
  5. Stir in the lime juice and zest.
  6. For a smoother soup, carefully blend part of the soup with an immersion blender. You can also leave it chunky if preferred.
  7. Stir in the coconut milk or milk and heat gently until warmed through. Taste and adjust seasoning as needed.
  8. Top with cilantro, scallions, and a pinch of garam masala before serving.

Serving Tip

Serve over rice or alongside bread and a salad for a simple meal.

Freezer Tip

Freeze extra soup in single portions for an easy meal another day.

Variation

This soup also works well with added chicken, fish, or shrimp. If you do not have garam masala, try a light pinch of cinnamon for a gentle warm spice.

Roasted Sweet Potato Rounds with Honey & Feta

Tender roasted sweet potatoes topped with creamy feta and a drizzle of honey. This simple dish makes an elegant side for fall and winter meals.

Serves: 2-4 as a side dish

Ingredients

  • 2 large sweet potatoes, peeled and sliced into ½-inch rounds
  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • Salt and black pepper, to taste
  • ¼ cup crumbled feta cheese
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • 1 tablespoon fresh parsley, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon fresh thyme leaves (optional)

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 400°F. Line a baking sheet with parchment paper.
  2. Arrange the sweet potato slices in a single layer on the prepared baking sheet.
  3. Drizzle with olive oil and season with salt and pepper. Toss gently to coat.
  4. Roast for 20–25 minutes, turning halfway through, until the sweet potatoes are tender and lightly golden.
  5. Remove from the oven and immediately sprinkle with the crumbled feta cheese.
  6. Drizzle the honey evenly over the warm sweet potatoes.
  7. Garnish with parsley and thyme, if using.

Serving Tip

Serve warm as a side dish with roasted chicken, pork, fish, or a hearty grain salad.

Variations

  • Sprinkle with a pinch of cinnamon for a sweeter flavor.
  • Add a light dusting of smoked paprika for a savory twist.
  • For a dairy-free version, omit the feta or substitute a plant-based cheese.

Gingerbread People

Classic spiced gingerbread cookies with a simple piped icing — a fun, creative snow day activity, decorated with your favorite sprinkles or candy.

Makes about 2 dozen cookies

Ingredients

  • 2¼ cups flour
  • ½ cup sugar
  • ½ cup molasses
  • 1 egg
  • ½ cup butter
  • 1 teaspoon each ground ginger and cloves
  • ½ teaspoon each ground cinnamon, cardamom, and nutmeg
  • 1 teaspoon baking soda
  • ½ teaspoon baking powder
  • ½ teaspoon salt

Icing

  • 1¾ cups confectioners' sugar
  • ⅛ teaspoon cream of tartar
  • ¼ cup water
  • Drop of vanilla extract

Instructions

  1. Preheat the oven to 350°F.
  2. Combine all the cookie ingredients in a bowl and mix on medium speed with a hand mixer, or use a wooden spoon, until well mixed. Form into a ball and refrigerate for an hour.
  3. Working on a lightly floured surface, cut the ball of dough into 4 pieces. Roll out to about ⅛-inch thickness.
  4. Cut into gingerbread people (or your favorite shapes) using cookie cutters.
  5. Using a spatula, transfer to a cookie sheet lined with parchment paper.
  6. Bake for about 8 minutes, watching closely minute by minute so they don't burn.
  7. Transfer to a wire rack and cool completely before icing and decorating.

For the Icing

In a small bowl, sift the confectioners' sugar and cream of tartar. Mix in the water and vanilla extract to form a smooth, creamy icing — adjust with more confectioners' sugar or water until it's slightly thinner than peanut butter, not so thin that it drips. Pipe onto the cooled gingerbread people and decorate with your favorite sprinkles or candy.

Cook's Note

If using raisins for decorating, press them into the raw dough before baking rather than adding them after.

Desfruta! Enjoy!

Keeping Local Food Year-Round

Saving seasonal foods helps you enjoy local produce from the Southcoast of Massachusetts all year long. These simple preservation methods make winter cooking easier, especially when fresh local produce is harder to find. Small, ready-to-use portions help reduce waste and make everyday meals faster and more enjoyable.

Freezing Vegetables

Freezing is one of the easiest ways to preserve vegetables like corn, peppers, squash, and green beans.

Basic steps:

  1. Cut vegetables into small, even pieces (about 1 cup portions works well for single servings).
  2. Bring a pot of water to a boil.
  3. Place vegetables in the boiling water for 1–2 minutes (this is called blanching).
  4. Drain immediately and place in a bowl of ice water to stop cooking.
  5. Drain well and pat dry.
  6. Store in labeled freezer bags or containers. Include the date.

Tip: Freeze in small portions so you can use just what you need for soups, sautés, or side dishes.

Freezing Fruit

Freezing fruit is an easy way to save the taste of summer for later in the year. It works well for berries, peaches, apples, and rhubarb.

Simple method:

  1. Wash fruit and remove any stems, pits, or cores.
  2. Cut larger fruit into bite-sized pieces.
  3. Spread fruit in a single layer on a baking sheet so it does not stick together.
  4. Freeze until solid (a few hours or overnight).
  5. Transfer frozen fruit to labeled freezer bags or containers. Include the date.

Tip: Frozen fruit can be used directly in baking, smoothies, oatmeal, or cooked into sauces without thawing first.

Drying Herbs

Drying herbs helps you keep summer flavors for months.

Simple method:

  1. Tie herbs (such as parsley, thyme, oregano, or rosemary) into small bundles.
  2. Place in a paper bag or hang in a dry, well-ventilated place away from sunlight.
  3. Let herbs dry completely until they feel crisp.
  4. Crumble into a clean jar and store tightly sealed.
  5. Keep in a cool, dark cabinet.

Tip: Dried herbs are stronger than fresh—use a smaller amount when cooking.

Freezing Herb Cubes (Easy Cooking Shortcut)

This is a great way to save herbs like basil, parsley, cilantro, or celery tops.

Steps:

  1. Chop herbs finely in a food processor.
  2. Add a small amount of olive oil (and a splash of water if needed) to form a thick paste.
  3. Spoon into ice cube trays and freeze overnight.
  4. Once frozen, transfer cubes to labeled freezer bags.
  5. Return to freezer.

How to use: Drop a cube directly into soups, sauces, or sautés while cooking.
